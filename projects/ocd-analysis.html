PROJECT 05 / HEALTH DATA × EXPLORATORY ANALYSIS

OCD Patient Analysis

An exploratory look at demographic, symptom, and treatment variables in a public dataset—built to practice asking focused questions and communicating results with care.

View GitHub project ↗View dataset source ↗

THE QUESTION

What patterns can this dataset help describe?

I explored a public OCD patient dataset containing demographic and clinical fields such as age, ethnicity, diagnosis year, symptom types, symptom scores, family history, and prescribed medication. The questions focused on the shape of the data and how its recorded variables appeared together.

THE APPROACH

Explore the records, then qualify the story.

I used PostgreSQL, Python, Excel, and visualization tools to examine distributions and comparisons. The analysis looked at age groups, obsession and compulsion scores, symptom categories, family-history patterns, diagnosis years, and medication counts.

  • Summarized age and diagnosis-year distributions.
  • Compared recorded symptom types and score averages.
  • Reviewed treatment and medication fields in the dataset.

WHAT I FOUND

Descriptive patterns—not clinical conclusions.

Within this dataset, the 41–50 age group had the largest representation, the average recorded compulsion and obsession scores were about 19.6 and 20.04 out of 40, and harm-related obsessions and washing compulsions were common categories. These are summaries of this particular dataset, not estimates of prevalence or evidence that ethnicity, family history, or any other factor causes a clinical outcome.

This project is for data-analysis practice only. Dataset limitations and potential sampling bias mean these observations should not guide diagnosis, treatment, or healthcare decisions.
