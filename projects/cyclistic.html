PROJECT 04 / MOBILITY × CUSTOMER BEHAVIOR

Cyclistic Bike-Share Case Study

A year of bike-share trips becomes a closer look at how casual riders and annual members use a Chicago mobility service—and what those differences could mean for membership growth.

View GitHub project ↗Explore Tableau dashboard ↗

THE QUESTION

How do members and casual riders use bikes differently?

This case study follows the Google Data Analytics Cyclistic scenario. The marketing team’s goal was to understand differences between annual members and casual riders, then use those patterns to develop thoughtful ways to encourage membership.

THE APPROACH

Explore trips across a full year.

I combined twelve monthly trip-data files covering December 2023 through November 2024, cleaned and prepared the data, and analyzed it using Python and PostgreSQL. Tableau visualizations compared usage by rider type and looked at service, distance, time of day, day of week, and month.

  • Prepared and combined a large public trip dataset with Python.
  • Used SQL to aggregate and compare ride patterns across customer types.
  • Built visual summaries to make the results easier to interpret.

WHAT IT SUGGESTS

Meet riders where their habits already are.

The analysis was designed to identify differences in when and how casual riders and members use the service. Those patterns can help shape targeted membership messaging—for example, at high-use locations or around the trip behaviors that distinguish occasional from regular riders. The recommendations are directional: trip records alone cannot identify individual riders or prove what would cause someone to subscribe.

The public dataset excludes personal information, so individual purchase histories, residency, and repeat-customer behavior cannot be linked.
